Part 1: Infusing Entrepreneurial Mindset in Higher Education


Published in the Winter / Spring 2024 edition

Summary

Higher education has traditionally focused on developing technical knowledge and disciplinary expertise, but employers increasingly expect graduates to possess transferable skills such as critical thinking, communication, teamwork, and problem solving. In this article, I explore how colleges can intentionally cultivate an entrepreneurial mindset across academic programs by connecting classroom learning to authentic, real-world experiences.

The article examines several practical approaches, including adapting West Virginia's Simulated Workplace model for higher education, creating student-led organizations that operate as businesses, developing campus business incubators, and partnering with local companies to provide authentic project experiences. Rather than treating entrepreneurship as a standalone discipline, these strategies integrate entrepreneurial thinking throughout the curriculum regardless of a student's major.

The article concludes that embedding entrepreneurial thinking throughout the curriculum not only prepares graduates for an evolving workforce but also increases student engagement by making coursework more immediately relevant and connected to real-world challenges.

Part 2: Implementing Entrepreneurial Mindset Culture Change


Published in the Spring / Summer 2024 edition

Summary

Building on the first article, this follow-up focuses on the organizational leadership required to successfully implement entrepreneurial thinking across an institution. Rather than viewing curriculum transformation as a purely academic initiative, the article approaches it as a structured organizational change process requiring stakeholder analysis, communication planning, and long-term assessment.

The article outlines practical strategies for evaluating stakeholder readiness, selecting appropriate change management approaches, aligning initiatives with institutional mission and strategic goals, and developing communication plans tailored to faculty, staff, and administrators. It emphasizes coaching, mentoring, and incremental adoption as key elements for sustainable organizational change.

Finally, the article discusses the importance of measuring outcomes, sharing lessons learned, and continuously refining implementation efforts. By treating institutional transformation itself as an entrepreneurial process, educational leaders can build lasting cultural change while improving student outcomes.
